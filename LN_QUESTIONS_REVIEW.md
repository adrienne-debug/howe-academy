### Passage 1 (~800L)

**Before:** Most people think of snakes as cold and *indifferent* animals, but keepers who spend real time with them describe something more interesting. Ball pythons appear to *distinguish* between familiar and unfamiliar handlers — they settle more quickly with people they regularly interact with. Hognose snakes are known for elaborate *theatrics*: when threatened, they flatten their necks, hiss loudly, and if that fails, roll onto their backs and play dead. Even the speed of a snake's tongue flick is *meaningful* — a slow, steady flick signals calm exploration, while rapid flickering indicates heightened alertness. These behaviors suggest that snakes respond actively to their environment in ways that reward patient observation.

**After:** Most people think of crows as noisy and *indifferent* birds, but scientists who watch them closely describe something more interesting. Even the rhythm of a crow's call is *meaningful* — a few slow caws keep the group in touch, while a burst of harsh calls gathers the flock to chase off an owl. These behaviors suggest that crows respond to their world in ways that reward patient observation.

### Passage 2 (~820L)

**Before:** When a snake *molts*, or sheds its skin, the process reveals far more than new scales. Shedding is a sign of health — a well-fed snake in good conditions sheds regularly, while one that is stressed or dehydrated often produces an incomplete shed.
