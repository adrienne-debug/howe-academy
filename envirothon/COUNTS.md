## The core flag

`core:true` = the card is named by a Learning Objectives line or by a 2026 Area 8 test question in COVERAGE_objectives.md.
Matching is by the exact card front as written in the coverage maps. ID decks: the objective lines name whole species sets, not
single cards, so ID cards are core by attribute — Wildlife: `· class`, `· food`, `· habitat` (the three every animal has on the
teacher's sheet); Aquatic: classification / family / habitat / adult food / native status / pollution-tolerance group; Forestry:
every tree card ("identifying features of species in the designated set"); Soils: the horizon cards ("Identify parts of a soil
profile"). CEI and the Appendix have no objectives sheet, so only their test-backed cards are core — the teacher may want to
treat the whole CEI Soil Health group as core. Nothing was dropped: `core:false` cards are the rest of the manuals' terms and facts.

## Why the set is bigger than the 900–1,300 the plan estimated

Nothing was padded, and nothing was cut to reach a round number (her rule and the brief). The estimate was made before the profiles were read. Three things pushed the count up:
- **Species profiles are dense.** The 30 standard wildlife profiles plus the 5 featured ones give 574 one-fact ID cards (13–21 per animal). The teacher's sheet asks for Description · Habitats and Habits · Classification · Food plus the ID essentials, and each of those had to be its own card so the back can be checked at a glance. Aquatics ID (5 fish, 4 plants, 29 macroinvertebrate taxa) gives 321.
- **Each manual's glossary terms are carded in that station.** Roughly 100–130 glossary terms per station are used in the manual, and each gets a card unless it is the wrong sense of the word.
- **The 2027 current-issues packet is 11 full articles** (311 cards). None of them is a summary sheet.

The `core` flag (above) is how the unit can start Lincoln on the objective-backed cards and hold the rest.

## Errata and source conflicts

- **Forestry Manual p.11, the CO2 erratum (club syllabus):** the edition on disk ("Updated August 2026") no longer prints "approximately 90%". It now says "a large volume". **No card was made from that sentence in either form.**
- **Sources that disagree with themselves:**
  - Card from the text figure, other figure in `info`: white pine needles (text 2.5–6 in. vs. table 3–5 in.).
  - Both kept, each with its own wording: light absorbed by 600 ft vs. about 100 ft; water has 30× less oxygen than air vs. under 2.5%.
  - One figure chosen (choice listed in the notes): Beaver, Bobcat, Chipmunk and Red-tailed Hawk numbers.
  - No card, because the figures are ambiguous: crappie spawning temperatures.
  - Details are in `parts/*_notes.md`.
- **Typos in the sources:** quoted verbatim in the review file, and spelled correctly on the card back. Examples: "lightening", "Reptilla", "2530 days" (Canada goose incubation, 25–30), "Sarcopterygill".

## Checks run

1. **Quote check, every card.** `check_quotes.py` confirms each card's saved source sentence appears word for word on the cited page: **0 problems on all cards in the parts**. The merge then removed cross-station duplicates (see `parts/merge_log.txt`).
2. **Test-wording check.**
   - No card copies a 2026 Area 8 test question.
   - Three fronts that read like test questions were reworded.
   - 16 cards share a 7-word run with a test only because the test quoted the manual. Each of those cards' source quotes contains the shared words.
3. **Random verification, first sample: PASS 41/50.** An independent agent read each card's page and failed 9:
   - an info line naming the wrong station (a merge bug, now fixed for every card);
   - a front saying "three words" over a four-word back;
   - a list cut short;
   - a front with two right answers;
   - "wet" written for "high water table";
   - an outcome list skipped;
   - "many factors including" turned into "the three factors";
   - a dropped "may";
   - a front that was unclear.
   All 9 fixed from the source (`parts/verify_result.md`).
4. **Pattern sweep after sample 1.** 117 cards matching those failure patterns were re-read against their pages: 73 kept, 42 fixed, 1 dropped (`parts/lint_fixes.md`).
5. **Random verification, second sample (50 different cards, after the sweep): PASS 43/50.** The 7 fails:
   - 4 dropped qualifiers: "most common members", "Average", "often", "including";
   - 1 reworded phrase ("exceptionally rare" → "very rare");
   - 1 front with two answers;
   - 1 circular definition.
   All 7 fixed from the source (`parts/verify_result2.md`).
6. **Qualifier sweep after sample 2.** 423 cards whose source quote holds a qualifier their back lacks were re-read against the page: 241 kept, 182 fixed, 0 dropped (`parts/hedge_fixes_A.md`, `parts/hedge_fixes_B.md`).
7. **Random verification, third sample (50 cards not sampled before, after both sweeps): PASS 40/50.** The 10 fails were mostly word-level drift from the source:
   - "lighter" for "light brown";
   - "block" for "interfere with";
   - an added "(stores)";
   - "break the wind" for "break the force of the wind";
   - a dropped "effectively" and a dropped "for part of their life cycle";
   - a glossary `info` missing "or nearly so";
   - one front with two answers;
   - one front with a wrong premise;
   - one wrong page.
   All 10 fixed from the source (`parts/verify_result3.md`).

**What the three samples mean.** Across 150 random cards, 124 passed. Every one of the 26 that failed is now fixed. Under this strict word-for-word standard, cards not yet sampled will still have a similar share of small wording drifts: a qualifier softened, a word swapped for a near-synonym, a list item shortened. None of the 26 was invented content: every fail had a source sentence behind it. That still breaks her "pure like definition and facts" rule, though, so **`REVIEW_cards.md` should be checked deck by deck before a deck is sent out**. It prints every card next to its exact source sentence for that purpose. A full card-by-card audit against the page (about 15 agent-runs of roughly 300 cards each) would remove most of the remaining drift; it was not run in this session.

### Forestry

I checked the `in` hints and also grepped every glossary headword against the manual, as the lead asked.

- **Arrangement:** the glossary sense is the wildlife-habitat element. The manual uses "arrangement" for leaves and twigs, plus one loose "in an arrangement" on p.12.

- **Cross Section:** the glossary sense is "a sample meant to represent a whole". The manual means a cut surface of a trunk.
- **Dissolved Oxygen (DO):** the count is inflated by "(DO)". The manual only says "dissolved nutrients".
- **Drainage Class (Natural):** this is a soils term. The manual only uses "drainage" in general.
- **Evapotranspiration, pH:** not in the Forestry manual.
- **Gas:** an everyday word. It is used once, in "atmospheric gas".
- **Naturalist:** only the proper name "Naturalist Center", on the resources page p.75 (not test material).
- **Point Source Pollution:** it appears only inside "non-point source pollution", which is carded.
- **Pools:** only "gene pool" (p.61), a different sense.
- **Scale:** the glossary sense is a fish scale. The manual means cone scales and the Biltmore stick scale.
- **Specialists:** only "forestry specialists" (people, p.7), a different sense.
- **Soil Consistence / Genesis / Reaction:** they matched only on the word "soil". These are soils terms not used here.
- **Tidal (Lunar) Cycles:** false match on "cycles".
